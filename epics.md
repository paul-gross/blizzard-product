# Epics

The registry of blizzard's live epics: the capability areas the product is built from, in priority order — the order of
the rows is the order of the plans behind them. An epic is a capability area, not a version, and it may land in slices.
Each epic serves one or more destinations in [milestones.md](./milestones.md); this is a reference list of what is being
built and why, not a status board — where an epic's slices actually stand is tracked in the milestone epic charts and in
GitHub, and a slice that has fully landed resolves in the epic chart that marks it `delivered`, then in
[delivered.md](./delivered.md) once the milestone it served is reached.

| `epic:mutation-testing`                 | Proof the tests would notice: mutation testing over the suites — seeded defects that must turn the gate red, run against a change's own lines at delivery time and across the whole corpus on a cadence — so a green gate means behavior is asserted, not merely executed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Operational necessity: an autonomous fleet acts on green without a human's doubt, so the suite's ability to fail is load-bearing. Scales the hand-run practice `bzh:mutation-review-selection` codifies. A [`milestone:homeostasis`](./milestones.md) spoke. | `epic:ideation`                         | The fleet as a source of product ideas, not only of code: on a cadence, it reads the software as built against the charter and offers the operator a short brief of high-level feature directions worth exploring — recommendations to weigh, never work it files for itself. Plan: [worker-lockdown](./plans/worker-lockdown.md).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `epic:adapters` (breadth slice)         | The harness seam with more than one occupant: OpenCode first, then Codex, as first-class workers behind the same execution boundary Claude Code rides — one runner advertising several harnesses, and graphs assigning deterministic acceptable harnesses to their session lineages.                                                                                                                                                                                                               | The Claude Code binding is recorded in [delivered.md](./delivered.md); this slice proves the seam with its second occupant and establishes the selection contract later occupants inherit. | `epic:fact-egress`                      | The fleet's facts on their way out: one row per step, per event, per attempt — names rather than ids, a timestamp on every row, and nothing pre-summed — written as files to a location the operator names, on a cadence they choose. Blizzard supplies the exit and no opinion about what waits on the other side, so an operator points Metabase, DuckDB, or a tool blizzard has never heard of at their own fleet's numbers without waiting on an endpoint built for their particular question. | Operational necessity: `epic:analytics` made the numbers reachable but not readable — today an answer costs a terminal, ten calls, and a scratch script that is thrown away, so the same question is paid for again every week. Ships no dashboard, no vendor adapter, and no loader; the tools that move files into warehouses already exist and are better at it. Proven when two independent tools are wired against the export from the outside with no blizzard change. A [`milestone:observability`](./milestones.md) spoke. Plan: [fact-egress](./plans/fact-egress.md). |
| `epic:tracing`                          | The fleet's own account of a night, as traces: every chunk's journey emitted over OpenTelemetry — node steps, gate outcomes, queue and lease waits, hub-executed deliveries — each span carrying the model, tokens, cost, and resolved choice it belongs to. One standard protocol to one configured endpoint, so every observability backend that exists, or comes to exist, works without blizzard shipping an adapter for any of them.                                                          | The harness cannot supply this: a coding agent knows a session rather than a chunk, exits before the gate judges its work, is absent entirely for hub-executed nodes, and in OpenCode's case emits nothing at all — so a harness-shaped view of the fleet fails the seam [`milestone:polyglot`](./milestones.md) exists to prove. Spans cannot be backfilled, so the record begins the day this lands. Retention and fan-out belong to the operator's collector, never to blizzard. A [`milestone:observability`](./milestones.md) spoke. Plan: [tracing](./plans/tracing.md).  |
| `epic:projects`                         | Project as a first-class grouping across the platform — of what to do and who does it: one hub hosts many projects, each with its own work sources and its work carrying its project from ingest to landing; one runner serves them all, hosting a local workspace per project. Three projects on a laptop means three workspaces and one runner, not three of everything.                                                                                                                         | Lands in two slices, hub then runner. A [`milestone:projects`](./milestones.md) spoke.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `epic:ci-feedback`                      | Routing CI and review results back into the owning session, capped feedback rounds.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Feeds the existing merge-queue machinery. Plan: [ci-feedback](./plans/ci-feedback.md).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `epic:intake`                           | The intake workbench: a screen of its own where queued work is assembled rather than pasted — the operator browses and searches the work sources' open items, pulls the chosen ones through ingest, and shapes what lands: many chunks selected at once, grouped, ungrouped, inspected side by side. The board's per-card grouping controls grow into a surface built for working a backlog down.                                                                                                  | The forge stays the backlog's home — this screen moves its items into the fleet with less friction, never defines work. Browsing means the work-source seam gains an optional enumerate/search capability, a deliberate loosening of id-addressed ingest decided here; a hub of many sources and projects reshapes the surface, so this follows `epic:projects`. Ungrouping asks of grouping what it cannot do today: splitting a folded chunk back apart.                                                                                                                      |
| `epic:ui-toolkit`                       | A component toolkit under the board instead of hand-rolled elements: a deliberate survey of the component libraries and developer-accelerating frontend frameworks the web surface does not yet use, one chosen on evidence, and the board's custom-built pieces migrated where the payoff is real — so the next small form or dialog is assembled from parts, not invented again.                                                                                                                 | Operational necessity: every small surface the board grows — a form, a picker, a sheet — is custom work to write, style, and maintain, and the count rises with each epic that touches the board (the story map, `epic:intake`, `epic:self-sourced-work`'s authoring surface). A survey-then-adopt effort in the spirit of `epic:config`.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `epic:config`                           | The configuration surface: every operational constant as config, comparable-run tuning for the harness-engineer persona.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | A survey-then-sweep effort rather than a large build.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `epic:chat`                             | Telegram bot (D-031): question notifications with one-tap answers, delivery confirmations — and the notification fan-out seam it is the first binding of.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | The seam is built with its first channel: who subscribes to what, and how a chat identity maps to a hub user, are decided here. Plan: [chat](./plans/chat.md).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `epic:pwa`                              | The board installed on a home screen — a progressive-web-app shell, laid out for a thumb — and question notifications surfacing through it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | One of two answers to the same need, weighed against `epic:android` and `epic:ios`: a wrapped page that installs cheaply, or native apps that earn the multi-hub view. Which one blizzard invests in is a deliberately deferred choice. Extracted from `epic:board` — a home-screen shell and a notification channel are packaging, not board surface. Question notifications ride the fan-out seam `epic:chat` builds.                                                                                                                                                         |
| `epic:android`                          | The fleet native on Android: a store-installed app where the operator registers every hub they follow — the way a Jira app registers its sites — and local runners alongside them, then reads it all as one: a unified view of chunks, questions, and escalations across fleets, with the platform's own notifications carrying the fleet's voice.                                                                                                                                                 | Enters as an exploration, a deliberate maybe: `epic:pwa` answers one hub from a phone, and what a native app must earn is the multi-hub view and the notification reliability a wrapped page cannot give. The hub-and-runner registry and the aggregation model are designed once, shared with `epic:ios`; whichever ships first decides them.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `epic:ios`                              | The fleet native on iOS: the same promise on Apple's platform — registered hubs and runners read as one unified view, reached through notifications that behave like the platform's own.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Enters as an exploration alongside `epic:android`, whose registry and aggregation design it shares; the platforms differ enough — store, notification plumbing, background rules — that each earns its own epic.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `epic:team`                             | Team mode: shared visibility and multi-operator policy over one hub-arbitrated fleet — cross-machine arbitration is the hub's queue, never forge-native acquisition.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Builds on the human-auth work (`epic:board`'s remote slice).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `epic:queue` (priority slice)           | The ready queue ordered by something better than the accident of arrival. An operator who knows one chunk matters more than the four ahead of it has no way to say so, and a chunk nobody champions sinks a little further every time newer work arrives. Priority makes the first sayable; aging makes the second impossible, so that having waited longest eventually counts for something.                                                                                                      | Operational necessity: arrival order is honest only while the queue drains as fast as it fills, and a fleet working through the night will outrun the operator feeding it. Unpromoted, and deliberately so — ordering earns its plan when the queue is deep enough that something can starve in it.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `epic:batching`                         | Execution units, the heuristic packer, the LLM planner, conflict packing, batch failure semantics.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Parked by design — captured, not scheduled. Plan: [batching](./plans/batching.md).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
