# Plan — `epic:steering`

The operator watches a chunk head somewhere wrong and has two options, both bad: watch the night go to waste, or take
the session over by hand. What they want is the thing they would do with a colleague — say one sentence and let them
carry on. This epic builds exactly that and nothing more: a word to a chunk already running. The narrow shape is the
point, because an open channel to a working agent is a different product, and the human-channel taxonomy stays small
enough to hold in the head.

## What to build

- **One message to a running chunk.** The operator writes a sentence; the agent receives it and continues. Not a
  conversation, not a takeover.
- **Delivered at the next natural pause.** The word arrives where the agent can act on it rather than mid-thought,
  riding the runner's existing at-most-once resume — the fleet already interrupts a dormant session with a
  system-authored message, so what this adds is a human author and the path that carries their words there.
- **Recorded as a fact.** A human entry into a running chunk is part of its history like every other human entry, so the
  morning's account of what happened includes what was said to it.
- **Reachable where the chunk is already watched.** The affordance lives on the chunk as the operator is looking at it,
  not on a separate screen they must go and find.

## Open questions

- What a natural pause is in each harness, since the answer is the whole delivery guarantee and no two harnesses expose
  it the same way.
- What becomes of a message for a chunk that finishes before it lands — dropped with a note, or surfaced as something
  the operator must decide about.
- Whether a second message queues behind the first or replaces it, given that an operator who is steering is usually
  correcting themselves.
