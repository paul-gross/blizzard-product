Sheet 1 — routines, and the act of running one

Why a top-level tab

The board watches work in flight; gardening is a standing discipline set up once and tuned over months. It sits beside Board rather than inside it, and the app shell above it is unchanged — same header, same nav, one more tab.

Three surfaces, three nouns

The sub-strip is the machinery's own shape: routines (what is declared), runs & findings (what was seen), proposals (what wants a decision). Only the third carries a hot count, because it is the only one that is waiting on a person.

The record is small on purpose

A name, a graph, a default scope, model and effort defaults. No strategy field — the strategy lives in the graph's preambles, and the deployment's own registry behind the routine's name. The panel shows the strategy as read-only prose to make that visible without implying the hub owns it.

Run takes exactly three options

Scope, mode, charge note. The dialog shows the charge preview as prose and as values, because that is how the work item carries it — and the preview is what stops the charge note from being mistaken for a template slot.

Delta is the platform's honesty, not the agent's

The mode row names the baseline revision the hub already recorded for this routine over this scope, and how many commits landed since. Pick a scope this routine has never swept and delta self-disables with the reason — there is nothing to subtract from.

Scope is a list you can leave

The picker offers the declared vocabulary and one escape hatch. The hatch is deliberately worded as a warning rather than a convenience: the hub stores names and resolves none, so a near-miss slug opens a second bucket that nothing will ever reconcile.

A blocked routine says so here

tests points at a standard nobody has written, so its one run escalated at survey rather than judging by its own taste. Run is refused at the surface with that reason, because the fix is outside blizzard entirely — somebody writes the standard.

The trend is the run's product

Inflow against outflow per week, with the axis's own measurement beside it. A clean run still owns a row: leaving it out would put a hole in the trendline exactly where the garden was healthiest.

Open — where routines are authored

New… and Edit are stubs. Whether a routine is authored in this tab, in the CLI, or declared as data alongside the graph it points at is not settled — the plan says both surfaces exist, and this sheet assumes the tab is the primary one.

Open — scope health as a first-class read

The scopes table shows “last swept” per scope, which is what makes rotating scoped runs legible. A routine with one never-swept scope is not converged, and nothing on this sheet yet says so in one word.
